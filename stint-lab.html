How the simulator calculates
  • Wear per lap = wear_rate_base x track tyre_wear_mult x tyre_profile multiplier. Supplier durability and driver tyre management also scale wear in the game; here they are neutral.
  • Grip = grip_base x (1 - grip_loss x wear) for lateral and longitudinal separately; the wear factor never goes below 0.55 (lateral) or 0.50 (longitudinal). Past wear_cliff_onset_pct, wear counts wear_cliff_mult times faster. As in the game's tyre_model.py, during a race the tyre effect of the first lap in each wear_quantum step (default 0.02 = 2% of tyre life) is reused until wear reaches the next step, so lap times fall in small steps for every compound; a compound without a cliff also rounds wear to the nearest step. Fuel still changes every lap.
  • Lap time comes from the game's own lap solver (realistic_physics.py), run on the track's micro-sectors for a car with every rating at 50. Each corner zone's speed cap is its target_speed_kmh x lateral grip x a mass factor; straights keep their target speed. Braking and acceleration rates (12 and 7 m/s2 at rating 50) are multiplied by longitudinal grip. The solver then accelerates out of each zone along the game's power curve with drag, brakes at the last moment for the next zone, and adds up the time in 1 m steps. Grip therefore changes corner speed one for one, not by its square root.
  • Fuel is mass only: acceleration x [1 + (800/total - 1) x 0.5], clamped 0.70-1.25; corner speed x [1 + (sqrt(800/total) - 1) x 0.5], clamped 0.84-1.08. Braking ignores mass. Total = dry chassis + engine + fuel.
  • "Car faster than a 50-rated car" is taken off every lap. It moves race totals, not the choice of strategy.
  • Race laps = 50 x lap_modifier rounded the way the game rounds (Python round: a half goes to the even number, so 80.5 is 80).
  • With several track files loaded, the app races one average track: race laps, length, tyre_wear_mult, fuel_consumption and pit loss are the mean of the tracks, and each lap time is the mean of every track's own game lap time for the same tyres and fuel. Use it to find a strategy that works across a season or a group of tracks.
  • Each stop costs the pit lane loss plus tyre service: on average 5.0 - 0.1316 x (chief mechanic pit-stop skill - 1) seconds, which the game rolls between 75% and 125% of that. Refuelling adds 0.15 s per kg. Crew mistakes (a few seconds, now and then) are left out.
  • Fuel per lap = fuel_consumption x FUEL_PER_LAP_CONSTANT / FUEL_REFERENCE_EFFICIENCY, for an engine with fuel efficiency 50 (the game multiplies by 1 - 0.25 x (efficiency - 50) / 50). A track file without fuel_consumption uses the game's estimate: 1 + (straight share - 0.25) x 0.6 + (1 - lap_modifier / 3.5) x 0.2.
  • Each plan is a sequence of compounds, one per stint, in any order. With "Optimal split" the stint lengths are searched exhaustively for the fastest legal race; "Equal stints" splits the laps evenly, as earlier versions did. A plan needs as many different dry compounds as the model's mandatory_compound_count.
  • Track water (mm) holds for the whole race. Each compound uses its wetness_states entry for that water level: grip base x lat_mult / long_mult and wear rate x wear_mult, as the game's tyre model does. Intermediate and wet tyres join the plans when the water is above 0. Assumed, not yet checked in the game code: a plan that uses an intermediate or wet tyre is exempt from mandatory_compound_count, and only dry compounds count towards it.
  • With refuelling each stint starts with its own fuel and pays refuel time at the stop; without it the car starts with the fuel for the whole race, whatever the tank size, and stops are for tyres only. Traffic and track position are not modelled, so multi-stop gains are best case.
